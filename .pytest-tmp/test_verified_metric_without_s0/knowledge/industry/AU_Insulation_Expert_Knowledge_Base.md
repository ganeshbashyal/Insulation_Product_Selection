## 2. ENERGY EFFICIENCY — THERMAL INSULATION

### 2.1 7-Star NatHERS Requirement (NCC 2022+)

All new Class 1 buildings must achieve a **minimum 7-star NatHERS rating** (up from 6 stars). This drove demand for higher R-value ceiling insulation (R5.0–R8.0).

### 2.2 Minimum Ceiling R-Values by Climate Zone (NCC 2022, Part 13.2)

R-values depend on: climate zone, roof ventilation (vented vs standard), reflective insulation under roof, under-roof insulation R-value, and solar absorptance (SA) of roof surface.

| Climate Zone | Description | Typical Min Ceiling R-Value Range |
|--------------|-------------|-----------------------------------|
| **1** | Hot humid summer, warm winter | R1.5–R4.5 (varies with SA, venting, reflective) |
| **2** | Warm humid summer, mild winter | R2.5–R4.0 |
| **3** | Hot dry summer, warm winter | R2.5–R4.0 |
| **4** | Hot dry summer, cool winter | R3.0–R3.5 |
| **5** | Warm temperate (Sydney, Perth, Adelaide) | R2.5–R3.0 |
| **6** | Mild temperate (Melbourne, Canberra) | R3.0–R4.0 |
| **7** | Cool temperate (Hobart, Tas, alpine fringe) | R3.5–R5.0+ |
| **8** | Alpine | R4.0–R5.0+ |

**Key rules:**
- R-Value of reflective insulation is NOT included in the declared R-value of bulk insulation.
- R-values listed are for the labelled, declared R-value of the insulation product.
- "X = not permitted" appears in some cells (e.g., dark roofs with no reflective insulation in hot zones).
- Solar absorptance (SA) of roof surface matters: light roofs (SA ≤ 0.23) allow lower R-values; dark roofs (SA > 0.64) require higher R-values.

### 2.3 Total R-Value & Thermal Bridging (AS/NZS 4859.2)

- **Product R-Value** = declared R-value of a single insulation product (tested per AS/NZS 4859.1).
- **Total R-Value** = sum of thermal resistances of ALL layers in a building element, including air films, airspaces, framing, cladding, plasterboard, etc.
- **Thermal bridging** must be accounted for — steel framing, timber studs, fixings, and penetrations create paths of least resistance for heat flow.
- AS/NZS 4859.2 uses the **parallel path (upper limit) + series path (lower limit) method**, with the design value = geometric mean of the two.
- NCC 2022+ requires Total R-Value calculations per AS/NZS 4859.2 for DtS compliance.
- **Section J (Volume One)** and **Part 13 (Volume Two)** both reference this.

### 2.4 Compliance Pathways

| Pathway | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Deemed-to-Satisfy (DtS)** | Follow prescriptive R-value tables and construction specifications |
| **Verification Method (JV3 / VURB)** | Whole-building energy modelling — proposed building must perform ≥ reference building |
| **Performance Solution** | Alternative solution demonstrating compliance with Performance Requirements via expert judgement, calculation, or testing |

### 2.5 Wall Insulation Requirements

- External walls: minimum Total R-Value varies by climate zone (typically R2.0–R2.8 for walls in zones 6–8).
- NCC 2022 introduced **thermal bridging mitigation** requirements, especially for steel-framed construction.
- Steel framing significantly degrades wall R-value (a 90mm stud wall with R2.5 batts may only achieve ~R1.8 Total R-Value due to thermal bridging).

### 2.6 Floor Insulation

- Suspended timber floors: minimum R-value varies by climate zone (typically R1.0–R2.0 in zones 6–8).
- Slab-on-ground edge insulation: **mandatory in zones 7 & 8** (R0.64 for zone 7, R1.0 for zone 8).
- Any slab with in-slab heating/cooling: minimum R1.0 perimeter slab edge insulation.

## 3. CONDENSATION MANAGEMENT

### 3.1 NCC 2022 Part F8 / Part 10.8

**Scope:** NCC 2022 applied to Class 2 SOUs and Class 4 parts. **NCC 2025 expanded** to Class 1, 2, 3, 4, and 9c (first time hotels, hostels, aged care explicitly covered).

### 3.2 Vapour Permeance Requirements (External Walls)

| Climate Zones | Min Vapour Permeance | AS 4200.1 Class | Notes |
|---------------|--------------------|-----------------|-------|
| **1–3** | No requirement | — | Hot/humid zones, outward drying less critical |
| **4–5** | ≥ 0.143 µg/N·s | **Class 3** (moderately permeable) | Sydney (Z5), inland NSW/VIC (Z4) |
| **6–8** | ≥ 1.14 µg/N·s | **Class 4** (highly permeable) | Melbourne (Z6), Hobart (Z7), Alpine (Z8) |

**AS 4200.1 Vapour Control Membrane Classes:**
- Class 1 = lowest vapour permeance (vapour barrier)
- Class 2 = low permeance
- Class 3 = moderate permeance (≥ 0.143 µg/N·s)
- Class 4 = high permeance (≥ 1.14 µg/N·s)

### 3.3 Key Construction Requirements

1. Pliable building membrane must comply with AS 4200.1 and be installed per AS 4200.2.
2. Membrane must be located on the **exterior side of the primary insulation layer**.
3. Any sarking-type material or insulation layer on the exterior side of primary insulation must also meet vapour permeance requirements.
4. **NCC 2025 (Zones 6–8):** External walls must incorporate a **drained and ventilated cavity** behind cladding.
5. If no pliable membrane installed (except single-skin masonry/concrete), a **drained cavity** must separate the primary water control layer from water-sensitive materials.
6. **Roof space ventilation** strengthened in NCC 2025, extended to zones 4 and 5.

### 3.4 Condensation Risk Principles

- **Interstitial condensation** = condensation within the building fabric (between layers).
- **Dew point** = temperature at which water vapour in air condenses. Insulation must keep surface temperatures above dew point.
- Open-cell insulation (glasswool, rockwool, polyester) = high vapour permeance (allows drying).
- Closed-cell insulation (polystyrene, PIR with foil facing) = low vapour permeance (can act as vapour barrier).
- In cool climates (zones 6–8), vapour flows from warm interior to cold exterior in winter. Vapour-permeable membranes on the exterior allow the wall to "dry to the outside."
- **Critical rule:** Never trap moisture between two vapour-impermeable layers.
- HVAC: Cold ductwork and chilled water pipes must have continuous vapour barriers. Condensation risk often demands thicker insulation than energy code minimums.

## 4. ACOUSTIC / SOUND INSULATION

### 4.1 Key Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Rw** | Weighted sound reduction index — single-number rating of airborne sound insulation (lab tested) |
| **Rw + Ctr** | Rw with spectrum adaptation term — adjusts for low-frequency (bass) sound; always lower than Rw alone |
| **Ctr** | Spectrum adaptation factor for low frequencies (negative number, typically -1 to -7) |
| **DnT,w** | Weighted standardised level difference — field-measured airborne sound insulation |
| **DnT,w + Ctr** | Field-measured airborne insulation with low-frequency adjustment |
| **Ln,w** | Weighted normalised impact sound pressure level (lab) — lower = better |
| **LnT,w** | Weighted standardised impact sound pressure level (field) — lower = better |
| **STC** | Sound Transmission Class (US equivalent of Rw, not used in NCC) |

### 4.2 NCC Sound Insulation Requirements

#### Class 2 & 3 Buildings (Volume One, Part F7)

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Walls between SOUs | Rw + Ctr ≥ 50 |
| Walls between SOU and plant room, lift, stairway, corridor, lobby, or different classification | Rw ≥ 50 |
| Door in wall separating SOU from corridor/public lobby | Rw ≥ 30 (concession) |
| Floor between SOUs or SOU and plant/corridor/etc. | Rw + Ctr ≥ 50 (airborne), Ln,w ≤ 62 (impact) |
| Class 9c floor between SOUs | Rw ≥ 45 |

**Field verification (if tested on site):**
- DnT,w + Ctr ≥ 45 (airborne)
- LnT,w ≤ 62 (impact)

#### Class 1 Buildings (Volume Two / Housing Provisions, Part 10.7)

| Wall Type | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Type A** — Wall between bathroom/sanitary/laundry/kitchen in one dwelling and habitable room (not kitchen) in adjoining dwelling | Rw + Ctr ≥ 50, plus impact sound resistance (discontinuous construction) |
| **Type B** — All other separating walls between adjoining Class 1 dwellings | Rw ≥ 45 |

- Separating walls must continue to the underside of the roof above, OR to a ceiling that provides the required sound insulation.
- **Discontinuous construction** = minimum 20mm cavity between 2 separate leaves; masonry uses resilient wall ties; non-masonry has no mechanical linkage between leaves except at periphery.
- Staggered stud walls with common top/bottom plates are NOT discontinuous construction.

### 4.3 Acoustic Design Principles

- **Mass law:** Doubling mass increases sound insulation by ~6 dB. Heavier walls = better airborne isolation.
- **Mass-spring-mass:** Two leaves of plasterboard separated by an air cavity with absorption = significantly better than single leaf. The cavity acts as a spring.
- **Cavity absorption:** Glasswool/rockwool/polyester batts in the cavity absorb resonant sound, improving Rw by 5–10 dB.
- **Decoupling:** Resilient mounts, staggered/double studs, or discontinuous construction break the vibration path.
- **Flanking paths:** Sound travels through gaps, penetrations, ducts, continuous ceilings/floors. Sealing is critical.
- **Low-frequency sound (Ctr):** Bass frequencies are harder to control — need mass, deep cavities, and decoupling. This is why Rw+Ctr is always lower than Rw.
- **Impact sound:** Footfall, dropped objects. Controlled by resilient underlayments, floating floors, discontinuous ceiling systems.
- **Acoustic insulation ≠ thermal insulation:** Acoustic batts are typically higher density (40–80 kg/m³ vs 10–14 kg/m³ for thermal glasswool). Density matters more than thickness for mid-frequency absorption.

### 5.1 Bulk Insulation

| Material | Description | R-Value Range | Key Properties |
|----------|-------------|---------------|---------------|

### 5.2 Reflective Insulation

| Material | Description | Key Properties |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Reflective foil laminate (RFL)** | Foil-faced building membrane/sarking | Reflects radiant heat (emissivity ≤ 0.05), requires adjacent airspace for R-value, acts as vapour barrier |
| **Foil-faced blankets** | Glasswool blanket with foil facing (e.g., Anticon, Permastop) | Combined bulk + reflective; used under metal roofs |
| **Foilboard** | Rigid panel with foil facing on EPS/PIR core | Reflective + some bulk; used in walls, under slabs, under floors |

**Reflective insulation R-values:**
- Depend on: emissivity of surfaces, number of airspaces, airspace dimensions, direction of heat flow, temperature difference.
- Must have an adjacent still airspace to provide thermal resistance.
- R-value is NOT included in the declared R-value of bulk insulation (per NCC).
- Electrically conductive — safety critical (AS/NZS 3000).

### 5.3 Rigid Foam Boards

| Material | Description | R-Value/m | Key Properties |
|----------|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| **PIR (polyisocyanurate)** | Rigid thermoset foam | R0.022–R0.026/m (≈R-value up to ~R5.0+ in 90mm) | Highest thermal performance per mm, fire-resistant, foil-faced (vapour barrier), commercial + residential |
| **XPS (extruded polystyrene)** | Closed-cell rigid foam | R0.025–R0.030/m | Waterproof, high compressive strength, under slabs, roofs, cavity walls |
| **EPS (expanded polystyrene)** | Bead foam | R0.030–R0.035/m | Lower cost than XPS, some moisture absorption, under slabs, cavity walls, panels |
| **PU (polyurethane)** | Spray foam | R0.024–R0.028/m | Seamless air seal, vapour barrier, expanding, requires professional installation |

### 5.4 Spray Foam

- **Open-cell spray foam:** R~0.030/m, vapour permeable, air-sealing, sound absorption, lower cost.
- **Closed-cell spray foam:** R~0.023/m, vapour barrier, structural rigidity, higher cost, higher R per mm.
- Niche in Australia compared to US/Canada; growing in commercial and premium residential.

## 8. THERMAL PRINCIPLES (FUNDAMENTALS)

### 8.1 Heat Transfer Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Description | Insulation Strategy |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Conduction** | Heat transfer through solid materials | Bulk insulation (trapped air pockets resist conduction) |
| **Convection** | Heat transfer via air movement | Bulk insulation (still air in pockets); air sealing to prevent convective loops |
| **Radiation** | Heat transfer via electromagnetic waves | Reflective surfaces (low emissivity foil reflects radiant heat) |

### 8.2 R-Value & U-Value

- **R-Value** = thermal resistance (m²·K/W). Higher = better insulation. R = thickness (m) / thermal conductivity λ (W/m·K).
- **U-Value** = thermal transmittance (W/m²·K). U = 1/R. Lower = better insulation.
- **Total R-Value** = sum of all layer resistances including air films, airspaces, and thermal bridging corrections.
- **Total System U-Value** = 1 / Total R-Value.
- **Declared R-Value** = manufacturer's labelled value per AS/NZS 4859.1.
- **Design R-Value** = adjusted for installation conditions (compression, temperature, ageing) per AS/NZS 4859.2.

### 8.3 Key Thermal Concepts

- **Thermal bridging** = heat flow through framing/penetrations bypassing insulation. Steel studs can reduce wall R-value by 40-60%.
- **Thermal mass** ≠ insulation. Thermal mass (concrete, brick) stores and releases heat; insulation resists heat flow. Both needed for good performance.
- **Air sealing** is as important as insulation. Gaps and draughts can reduce insulation effectiveness by 20-50%.
- **Compression reduces R-value.** A batt compressed to 80% of its thickness loses ~20% of its R-value.
- **Temperature dependence:** Some insulation (particularly reflective) has different R-values at different mean temperatures. AS/NZS 4859.1 specifies testing conditions.
- **Ageing/degradation:** PIR foam R-values can decline over time due to blowing agent diffusion (thermal drift). Foil facings slow this.

### 8.4 Insulation & Building Envelope Strategy by Climate

| Climate Zone | Strategy |
|--------------|----------|
| **1–3 (Hot)** | Focus on reflective insulation, radiant heat rejection, ventilation; bulk insulation if air-conditioned; light-coloured roofs (SA ≤ 0.23) |
| **4–5 (Warm temperate)** | Balanced bulk + reflective; ceiling R2.5–R4.0; wall R2.0–R2.5; manage summer heat gain + winter heat loss |
| **6 (Mild temperate)** | Higher bulk insulation; ceiling R3.0–R4.0; wall R2.5+; condensation management critical (Class 4 membrane) |
| **7–8 (Cool/Alpine)** | Maximum bulk insulation; ceiling R4.0–R5.0+; wall R2.5–R2.8+; slab edge insulation mandatory; drained & ventilated cavities; Class 4 membrane |

## 9. ACOUSTIC PRINCIPLES (FUNDAMENTALS)

### 9.1 Sound Basics

- **Sound** = pressure waves travelling through air (or structure). Measured in decibels (dB).
- **Frequency** = pitch (Hz). Human hearing: 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz. Speech: 250–4000 Hz. Bass: 20–250 Hz.
- **Decibels (dB)** = logarithmic scale. 10 dB increase = perceived as roughly "twice as loud." 3 dB change = just noticeable.
- **Sound insulation** = ability of a building element to reduce sound transmission.

### 9.2 Airborne vs Impact Sound

| Type | Description | Control Method |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Airborne** | Sound travels through air (speech, TV, music) | Mass, sealing, cavity insulation, decoupling |
| **Impact** | Sound generated by physical impact (footsteps, dropped items) | Resilient layers, floating floors, discontinuous construction |

### 9.3 Improving Sound Insulation — Hierarchy of Effectiveness

1. **Seal all gaps** (even 1% gap area can reduce Rw by 5–10 dB) — most cost-effective
2. **Increase mass** (heavier plasterboard, double layers) — +6 dB per doubling
3. **Add cavity absorption** (glasswool/polyester batts in cavity) — +5–10 dB
4. **Decouple** (resilient channels, staggered/double studs, discontinuous) — +5–15 dB
5. **Increase cavity depth** — improves low-frequency performance
6. **Avoid flanking paths** — seal penetrations, offset outlets, break continuous ceilings/floors

### 9.4 Acoustic Insulation Density Guide

| Application | Recommended Density | Typical Products |
|-------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| General acoustic walls | 10–14 kg/m³ (standard glasswool) | Earthwool, Pink Batts, Bradford Gold |
| Enhanced acoustic (party walls) | 30–40 kg/m³ | Soundscreen, Soundbreak, Sound Shield |
| Premium acoustic / studio | 40–80 kg/m³ | ROCKWOOL Safe'n'Sound, Quietspace |
| Impact sound (floors) | Resilient layer, not just density | Resilient underlay, floating floor systems |

## 10. INSTALLATION STANDARDS & BEST PRACTICE

### 10.1 Key Installation Standards

- **AS 3999:2015** — Bulk thermal insulation installation
- **AS 4200.2:2017** — Pliable building membrane installation
- **AS/NZS 3000:2018** — Electrical safety (foil insulation, cable derating)

### 10.2 Critical Installation Rules

1. **No gaps** — batts must fit snugly; gaps can reduce performance by 20-50%.
2. **No compression** — compressed batts lose R-value; maintain specified thickness.
3. **Overlap wall/ceiling** — ceiling insulation must overlap wall by ≥ 50mm where no wall insulation exists.
4. **Vapour management** — membranes on exterior side of primary insulation; correct vapour permeance class for climate zone.
5. **Electrical safety** — AS 3999 requires electrical safety assessment before insulation; cables may need derating; foil must be secured with non-conductive fasteners; clearance around light fittings/flues.
6. **Moisture** — insulation must be kept dry during storage and installation.
7. **Thermal bridging** — insulation should be continuous; consider thermal breaks at steel framing.
8. **Certified installers** — EEC Certified Insulation Installer program; required for some government programs.

### 10.3 Common Installation Failures

- Gaps around penetrations (downlights, exhaust fans, flues)
- Compressed batts in narrow cavities
- Foil installed on top of ceiling joists near electrical cables (safety risk)
- Vapour-impermeable membrane on wrong side of wall (condensation risk)
- Insulation not continued over wall plate (thermal bypass at ceiling/wall junction)
- Recessed downlights without covers (air leakage + fire risk + insulation gap)

## 11. BUSHFIRE (AS 3959:2018)

- BAL (Bushfire Attack Level) ratings: BAL-Low, BAL-12.5, BAL-19, BAL-29, BAL-40, BAL-FZ (Flame Zone)
- Insulation in bushfire-prone areas must comply with AS 3959 requirements
- Non-combustible insulation (glasswool, rockwool) generally suitable for all BAL levels
- Combustible insulation (polystyrene, polyester) may have restrictions at higher BAL levels
- Sarking/membranes must meet specific flammability indices for BAL-rated construction

## 14. QUICK REFERENCE — COMMON SCENARIOS

### "What R-value ceiling insulation do I need for a new home in Sydney?"
→ Climate Zone 5. Check NCC Table 13.2.3f. Typically R2.5–R3.0 minimum, but 7-star NatHERS assessment may require R4.0–R5.0+ depending on whole-of-home design. Always confirm with energy assessor.

### "What wall insulation for a Melbourne home?"
→ Climate Zone 6. Wall R-value typically R2.0–R2.5 minimum (Total R-Value). Use vapour-permeable wall wrap (Class 4, ≥1.14 µg/N·s). Consider thermal bridging if steel frame. Bradford Gold R2.5 or Earthwool R2.5 wall batts are common.

### "Sound insulation between apartments?"
→ Class 2 building, Part F7. Walls between SOUs: Rw + Ctr ≥ 50. Floors: Rw + Ctr ≥ 50, Ln,w ≤ 62. Use discontinuous construction or tested systems. Acoustic-grade cavity insulation essential.

### "Condensation risk in Hobart?"
→ Climate Zone 7. External walls need Class 4 vapour-permeable membrane (≥1.14 µg/N·s). NCC 2025 requires drained and ventilated cavity. Never use foil sarking on exterior side of insulation in this climate. Consider Tasmanian Designers' Guide (CBOS).

### "Best insulation for a metal roof in Brisbane?"
→ Climate Zone 2. Use foil-faced glasswool blanket (Anticon or Permastop) directly under metal roofing for combined radiant + condensation control. Ceiling batts R2.5–R4.0 depending on energy assessment. Ensure roof space ventilation.
