# Condensation Management — Compliance Digest

**Source:** ABCB *Condensation in buildings handbook* (Version 4.0, May 2026, aligned to NCC 2025)
**Raw text:** `compliance/raw/condensation_handbook.txt`

---

## 1. What condensation is (NCC defined term)

> **Condensation** = the formation of liquid water either on the surface of a building element/material, or within the building fabric, as a result of moist air coming into contact with a surface at a lower temperature.

Two types:
- **Surface condensation** — visible, on interior surfaces (windows, walls, cold spots).
- **Interstitial condensation** — hidden, within the building fabric (between layers). This is the NCC's main regulatory focus.

**Why it's getting worse:** modern buildings are more airtight and more highly insulated. Less heat flows through the fabric → reduced drying capacity → moisture persists longer. Traditional "rules of thumb" are no longer sufficient.

---

## 2. NCC scope & where the requirements live

Condensation management applies to:
- **Class 1** buildings (houses)
- **Sole-occupancy units (SOUs)** in Class 2 buildings (apartments)
- **Class 4** parts of buildings

NCC 2025 expanded scope to also cover **Class 3** (hotels/motels) and **Class 9c** (aged care) — first time these are explicitly covered.

Requirements located in:
| Document | Clause |
|----------|--------|
| NCC Volume One | **Part F8** (Class 2, 3, 4, 9c) |
| NCC Volume Two | **Part H4** (Class 1) — H4P7, H4D9 |
| ABCB Housing Provisions | **Part 10.8** (referenced by H4D9) |

- **H4P7** (Volume Two): same wording (applies to Class 1)

## 4. Verification Method (Performance Solution path)

**F8V1 / H4V5** — Condensation management:
Compliance is verified when a roof or external wall assembly does **not** develop a **mould index greater than 3** (per Section 6 of AIRAH DA07) on:
1. the interior surface of the water control layer, or
2. the surface of building fabric components interior to the water control layer.

Calculation must use:
- Input assumptions per **AIRAH DA07** (2021)
- The **intermediate method** for indoor design humidity (Section 4.3.2 of DA07)

**Mould Growth Index (MGI):** 0–6 scale. MGI = 3 means visible mould on <10% of surface (or up to 50% under microscope). Below 3 = only microscopically detectable.

**Mould sensitivity classes** (from DA07): glass/metal = low sensitivity; untreated wood = very sensitive (nutrients support growth).

## 5. DTS Provisions — External Wall Construction (F8D3 / 10.8.1)

### 5.1 Control layer requirements

1. A pliable building membrane or sarking-type material must comply with **AS 4200.1** and be installed per **AS 4200.2**.
2. It must be located on the **exterior side of the primary insulation layer**.
3. Any pliable membrane, sarking, or insulation layer on the exterior side of the primary insulation must meet a minimum **vapour permeance** (see table below).

### 5.2 Vapour permeance requirements by climate zone

| Climate Zones | Min vapour permeance | AS 4200.1 Class | Rationale |
|---------------|--------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| **1–3** | No requirement | — | Hot/humid; lower permeance helps limit inward moisture drive |
| **4–5** | ≥ **0.143 µg/N·s** | **Class 3** (moderate) | Sydney (Z5), inland NSW/VIC (Z4) |
| **6–8** | ≥ **1.14 µg/N·s** | **Class 4** (high) | Melbourne (Z6), Hobart (Z7), Alpine (Z8) — highest condensation risk |

**Vapour permeance** is measured in µg/N·s, tested per **ASTM-E96 Procedure B (Water Method)** at 23°C, 50% RH.

**AS 4200.1 Vapour Control Membrane (VCM) classes:**
- Class 1 = lowest permeance (vapour barrier)
- Class 2 = low
- Class 3 = moderate (≥ 0.143 µg/N·s)
- Class 4 = high (≥ 1.14 µg/N·s)

### 5.3 Drained & ventilated cavity (NCC 2025 change)

- **Climate zones 6–8:** external walls **must** incorporate a **drained and ventilated cavity** behind the cladding.
- If no pliable membrane is installed (except single-skin masonry/concrete), the primary water control layer must be separated from water-sensitive materials by a **drained cavity**.

### 5.4 Material behaviour (key principle)

- **Open-cell insulation** (mineral wool, fibreglass, polyester) = **high vapour permeance** (allows drying).
- **Closed-cell insulation** (polystyrene, PIR) = **low vapour permeance**.
- **Foil-faced insulation** = low vapour permeance (can act as a vapour barrier).

## 6. Roof Space Ventilation (F8D5/F8D6, 10.8.3/10.8.4)

- NCC 2025 strengthened roof space ventilation requirements and **extended them to climate zones 4 and 5** (previously only 6–8).
- Separate provisions apply depending on whether the **primary insulation layer is parallel or not parallel to the roof plane**:
  - Parallel (e.g., cathedral/raked ceilings, sarking under roof) → different ventilation rules
  - Not parallel (flat ceiling with attic space) → different rules

## 7. Condensation concepts (fundamentals)

### 7.1 Key definitions
- **Relative humidity (RH):** amount of water vapour in air relative to max it can hold at that temperature.
- **Dew point:** temperature at which air becomes saturated and water vapour condenses.
- **Saturation:** air at 100% RH.
- **Vapour pressure:** pressure at which vapour is in equilibrium with its condensed state.
- **Vapour permeance:** degree water vapour can diffuse through a material (µg/N·s).
- **Water control layer:** a pliable building membrane, or the exterior cladding where no membrane is present.
- **Primary insulation layer:** the most interior insulation layer of a wall or roof construction.

### 7.2 Sources of water vapour in buildings
Occupant activities (breathing, cooking, bathing, washing, drying clothes, unflued gas heaters), construction moisture (concrete, plaster, timber), and outdoor air.

### 7.3 Health & mould risk
- Mould growth risk rises above ~70–80% RH sustained over time.
- Health impacts: respiratory issues, allergies, asthma exacerbation.
- Comfort: high RH feels clammy; low RH causes dry skin/static.

### 7.4 Water vapour diffusion
- Vapour moves from **high to low vapour pressure** (warm/humid → cold/dry).
- In cool climates (zones 6–8), winter vapour drive is **from interior to exterior** → need vapour-permeable exterior layers so walls can "dry to the outside."
- In hot/humid climates (zones 1–2), vapour drive can be **from exterior to interior** → lower permeance exterior layers limit inward drive.

### 7.5 Climate & condensation potential
- Cooler climates (higher diurnal range, dew points) = higher condensation risk.
- Design goal: keep surface temperatures **above dew point**.

## 8. Creating dry buildings — design principles

### 8.1 Control layers (the "perfect wall" concept)
1. **Water control layer** (drainage plane) — sheds bulk water.
2. **Air control layer** — stops air leakage (carries ~100x more moisture than diffusion).
3. **Vapour control layer** — controls vapour diffusion (climate-dependent permeance).

### 8.2 Air control layers
- Air leakage is the dominant moisture transport mechanism (convective moisture transfer far exceeds diffusion).
- Sealing gaps, penetrations, and junctions is critical.

### 8.3 Water vapour control layers
- Placement and permeance depend on climate and wall assembly.
- Never trap moisture between two impermeable layers.

### 8.4 Risk assessment methods
- **AIRAH DA07:2021** — criteria for moisture control design analysis.
- **BS 5250:2021** — management of moisture in buildings (code of practice).
- **BS EN 15026:2023** — hygrothermal performance, numerical simulation.
- **ISO 13788:2012** — simplified hygrothermal calculation.
- Hygrothermal software (e.g., WUFI) for interstitial condensation risk.

## 9. HVAC condensation (commercial)

- Cold ductwork and chilled water pipes must have **continuous vapour barriers** on all insulation.
- Pipe/duct surface temperature must stay **above ambient dew point**.
- Condensation risk often demands **thicker insulation than energy code minimums**.
- Closed-cell elastomeric foam has integrated vapour barrier; mineral wool needs separate foil-facing with sealed/taped joints.

---

## 10. Design & construction tips (from handbook)

**Design:**
- Identify local climate (ABCB Climate Zone map) first.
- Select membranes/insulation as part of a construction *system*, not in isolation.
- Consider building use and future use changes (condensation risk changes with occupancy).
- Ensure surface temperatures stay above dew point.
- Plan for moisture egress over the building's life.

**Construction:**
- Install membranes per AS 4200.2 and manufacturer instructions.
- Seal openings (windows, doors) properly.
- Keep insulation **dry at all times** during storage and installation.
- Verify vapour-permeable membranes are on the correct side (exterior of primary insulation).
