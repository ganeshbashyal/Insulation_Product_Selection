# Sound Transmission & Insulation — Compliance Digest

**Source:** ABCB *Sound Transmission and Insulation in Buildings Handbook* (Version 4.3, June 2024, aligned to NCC 2022)
**Raw text:** `compliance/raw/sound_handbook.txt`

---

## 1. Scope & objective

**Objective (F7O1 / H4O6):** safeguard occupants from illness or loss of amenity from undue sound transmitted:
- between adjoining dwellings/units with sleeping facilities
- from common spaces into adjoining units
- from parts of a different classification into adjoining units

**Scope limits:** NCC only covers sound insulation *between* dwellings/units. It does NOT cover:
- external noise (traffic, trains, aircraft, industry)
- environmental noise emission from the building
- plant/appliance noise within a unit
- room acoustic design for home theatre
- noise transfer within a unit

Applies to **Class 1, 2, 3, and 9c** buildings.

## 4. Key acoustic ratings

| Symbol | Meaning | Lower/upper = better |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| **Rw** | Weighted sound reduction index (airborne, lab) | higher = better |
| **Rw + Ctr** | Rw with low-frequency spectrum adaptation | higher = better |
| **Ctr** | Spectrum adaptation term for low frequencies (negative) | — |
| **DnT,w** | Weighted standardised level difference (airborne, field) | higher = better |
| **DnT,w + Ctr** | Field airborne with low-frequency adjustment | higher = better |
| **Ln,w** | Weighted normalised impact sound pressure level (lab) | lower = better |
| **LnT,w** | Weighted standardised impact sound pressure level (field) | lower = better |

Determined per **AS/NZS ISO 717.1** (airborne) and **AS ISO 717.2** (impact).

## 5. Volume One requirements (Class 2, 3, 9c)

### 5.1 Class 2 & 3 (apartments & hotels)

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Wall between SOUs | **Rw + Ctr ≥ 50** |
| Wall between SOU and plant room, lift shaft, stairway, public corridor, public lobby, or different classification | **Rw ≥ 50** |
| Floor between SOUs, or SOU and plant/corridor/etc. | **Rw + Ctr ≥ 50** (airborne) AND **Ln,w ≤ 62** (impact) |
| Door in wall separating SOU from corridor/public lobby | **Rw ≥ 30** (concession) |

**Impact sound** is only a consideration for walls separating a bathroom/sanitary/laundry/kitchen from a habitable room in an adjoining SOU, or walls separating an SOU from a plant room or lift shaft.

**Field verification** (if tested on site): DnT,w + Ctr ≥ 45 (airborne), LnT,w ≤ 62 (impact).

### 5.2 Class 9c (residential care / aged care)

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Floor between SOUs | **Rw ≥ 45** (airborne) |
| Wall separating SOUs, or SOU from kitchen/bathroom/sanitary/laundry/plant/utilities | **Rw ≥ 45** |
| Impact sound (walls) | Only for walls separating SOU from kitchen or laundry |

### 5.3 Discontinuous construction

A wall required to have impact sound insulation must be **discontinuous construction**:
- minimum **20 mm cavity** between 2 separate leaves
- masonry: connected only with **resilient (acoustic) wall ties**
- non-masonry: **no mechanical linkage** between leaves except at periphery
- **Staggered stud walls with common top/bottom plates are NOT discontinuous construction**

## 6. Volume Two requirements (Class 1)

### 6.1 Separating walls between adjoining dwellings

| Wall type | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Type A** — wall between bathroom/sanitary/laundry/kitchen in one dwelling and habitable room (not kitchen) in adjoining dwelling | **Rw + Ctr ≥ 50** + impact sound resistance (discontinuous construction) |
| **Type B** — all other separating walls | **Rw ≥ 45** |

- Separating walls must continue to the **underside of the roof above**, OR to a **ceiling that provides the required sound insulation**.
- Insulation required for parts of wall common to adjoining dwellings, but **not** parts in the subfloor.

### 6.2 Habitable vs non-habitable rooms

**Habitable:** bedroom, living, lounge, music, TV, kitchen, dining, sewing, study, playroom, family room, home theatre, sunroom.

**Non-habitable:** bathroom, laundry, water closet, pantry, walk-in wardrobe, corridor, hallway, lobby, darkroom, clothes-drying room.

## 7. Design practice — improving sound insulation

### 7.1 Hierarchy of effectiveness
1. **Seal all gaps** (1% gap area can reduce Rw by 5–10 dB) — most cost-effective
2. **Increase mass** (+6 dB per doubling of mass)
3. **Add cavity absorption** (glasswool/polyester batts) — +5–10 dB
4. **Decouple** (resilient channels, staggered/double studs, discontinuous) — +5–15 dB
5. **Increase cavity depth** — improves low-frequency performance
6. **Avoid flanking paths** — seal penetrations, offset outlets, break continuous elements

### 7.2 Party walls (between SOUs)
- Balance acoustic performance vs minimal thickness/weight/cost.
- Thicker/heavier OR larger cavities with moderate cladding = better performance.
- Lightweight construction: thinner leaves + insulation-filled cavities.

### 7.3 Floors & ceilings
- Some floor designs **rely on carpet + underlay** to meet impact requirements.
- Substituting carpet with hard flooring can fail compliance → need acoustically-rated ceiling below.
- Break floorboards under party walls to isolate horizontally.

### 7.4 Services & penetrations (critical)
- Ducts penetrating sound-rated walls need acoustic treatment.
- Electrical outlets: sound-rated or surface-mounted; boxes airtight, sealed with acoustical caulk; **offset** outlets (not back-to-back).
- Downlights, ventilation grilles, ceiling speakers, fire sprinklers in sound-rated ceilings need acoustic detail.

### 7.5 Structure-borne sound
- Decouple mechanical equipment from structure (resilient mounts).
- Avoid short-circuit bracing between separate stud systems.
- Don't fix wall-mounted furniture across discontinuous walls.

---

## 8. Construction guidance

- **Windows/external doors:** acoustic glazing, seals for external noise (though NCC doesn't regulate external noise, good practice).
- **Penetrations/gaps:** seal all — biggest single factor in real-world performance.
- **Floors/ceilings:** resilient underlay, floating floors, discontinuous ceiling.
- **Walls:** discontinuous construction, acoustic cavity insulation, resilient ties.
- **Services:** offset outlets, seal boxes, acoustic treatment around ducts.

---

## 9. Refurbishing existing buildings

- Adding mass (extra plasterboard layer), cavity insulation, sealing gaps.
- **Substitution of materials:** replacing carpet with hard flooring can reduce acoustic performance — may require acoustically-rated ceiling below (install at construction time, as retrofit access is not guaranteed).

---

## 10. Acoustic testing & specification

- **Laboratory vs field testing:** lab results (Rw) are typically higher than field (DnT,w) due to flanking in real buildings.
- Test certificates should state: system tested, test conditions, background sound effects, flanking paths, reverberation times, third-octave results, precision.
- Prefer valid test certificates on the *system* plus clear installation requirements.
