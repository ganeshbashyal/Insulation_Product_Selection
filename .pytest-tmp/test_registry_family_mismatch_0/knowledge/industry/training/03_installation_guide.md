# Installation Guide — Best Practice & Common Mistakes

*Detailed installation guidance per AS 3999, AS 4200.2, AS/NZS 3000, and manufacturer instructions.*

---

## 1. Key installation standards

| Standard | Scope |
|----------|-------|
| AS 3999:2015 | Bulk thermal insulation — installation |
| AS 4200.2:2017 | Pliable building membranes — installation |
| AS/NZS 3000:2018 | Electrical safety (foil, cable derating) |
| Manufacturer instructions | Product-specific |

---

## 2. Golden rules of installation

1. **No gaps** — batts must fit snugly. Gaps reduce performance 20–50%.
2. **No compression** — compressed batts lose R-value. Maintain specified thickness.
3. **No moisture** — keep insulation dry during storage and installation.
4. **Correct vapour management** — membranes on exterior side of primary insulation.
5. **Electrical safety** — assess before install; derate cables; foil = conductive.
6. **Continuous envelope** — insulation must be continuous over the conditioned space.

---

## 3. Ceiling insulation

### Correct
- Fit batts snugly between joists, butted together (no gaps).
- Overlap wall by ≥50mm where no wall insulation.
- Keep clearance around downlights, flues, exhaust fans (per manufacturer + AS/NZS 3000).
- Lay batts to full thickness (don't compress under trusses).

### Common mistakes
- Gaps around penetrations (downlights, exhaust fans, flues).
- Compressing batts in shallow spaces.
- Insulation over recessed downlights (fire risk + air leakage).
- Not continuing insulation over wall plates (thermal bypass).

---

## 4. Wall insulation

### Correct
- Fit batts between studs, full cavity depth.
- Use vapour-permeable wall wrap on exterior side (Class 3/4 per climate zone).
- Ensure continuous insulation (no voids behind services).
- For steel frame, add thermal breaks.

### Common mistakes
- Compressing batts behind services/cables.
- Vapour-impermeable membrane on wrong side (condensation).
- Gaps at top/bottom of cavity.
- No thermal break on steel framing.

---

## 5. Underfloor insulation

### Correct
- Secure batts/panels between joists (staples, saddles, clips).
- Use moisture-resistant product (polyester, EPS, foil).
- Protect cables (WireGuard for EPS panels).
- Maintain ventilation of subfloor space.

### Common mistakes
- Insulation sagging/falling out.
- Blocking subfloor ventilation.
- Unprotected cables contacting conductive foil.

---

## 6. Reflective foil insulation — electrical safety (critical)

- Foil is **electrically conductive** — a serious electrical hazard.
- **Never** install foil over ceiling joists where cables are present.
- **Never** install foil near light fittings that penetrate the ceiling.
- Use **non-conductive (non-metallic) staples**.
- AS 3999 requires a **competent person** to do an electrical safety assessment before installation.
- Cables surrounded by insulation may need **derating** (reduced current capacity).

---

## 7. Membranes & sarking

- Comply with AS 4200.1 (material) and AS 4200.2 (installation).
- Locate on **exterior side of primary insulation**.
- Vapour permeance class per climate zone (Class 3 zones 4–5, Class 4 zones 6–8).
- Lap and tape joints per manufacturer.
- Don't leave exposed to UV beyond manufacturer limit (e.g., 60 days for Watergate Plus).

---

## 8. Certified installers & programs

- **EEC Certified Insulation Installer (CII)** — national certification.
- Required for many government programs (VEU, SHEPI, ACT schemes).
- Pre-installation electrical safety assessment required.
- Digital tools increasingly used for compliance monitoring.

---

## 9. Installation failure impact summary

| Failure | Impact |
|---------|--------|
| 1% gap area | R-value reduced 5–10% (acoustic: Rw −5–10 dB) |
| 20% compression | ~20% R-value loss |
| Wet insulation | 50–90% R-value loss |
| Thermal bridge (steel) | 40–60% wall R-value loss |
| Vapour barrier wrong side | Condensation + mould risk |
| Foil over cables | Electrical shock/fire hazard |
