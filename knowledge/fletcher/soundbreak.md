# Fletcher Soundbreak Acoustic Insulation

## Purpose of this file

This is the canonical internal description for Fletcher Soundbreak. It aligns the language used by the Streamlit demonstration, future Aircall content, sales staff and the product-master dataset.

The demo may recommend the **Soundbreak family** when the customer describes airborne sound transfer through a suitable residential wall, ceiling or midfloor and cavity insulation is an appropriate product role. It must not automatically choose an R-value, thickness, width, quantity or complete construction.

A human reviewer must confirm the available cavity, framing centres, complete building system, acoustic target, thermal requirement, moisture conditions, fire/BAL requirements, product code and stock before quoting.

## Canonical description

Fletcher Soundbreak is a range of high-density, flexible and resilient glasswool batts designed to provide acoustic absorption within framed residential walls, ceilings and midfloors. The batts also provide documented material thermal resistance.

Soundbreak works primarily as porous absorptive insulation within a cavity. It helps control cavity resonance and reduces sound energy within a correctly designed construction. It is not a flexible mass barrier, resilient mounting system, impact underlay, exposed room-acoustic panel or complete wall/floor/ceiling system.

Installed performance depends on the complete assembly: framing, linings, cavity depth, batt fit, resilient elements, junctions, doors, glazing, penetrations, services, sealing, flanking paths and workmanship.

## Current manufacturer-supported facts

- Product type: high-density glasswool acoustic and thermal batt.
- Current manufacturer application: residential internal and external walls, ceilings and midfloors.
- Nominal density range stated in the TDS: 24–32 kg/m³.
- Current material R-value range: R1.7 to R3.1 m²·K/W.
- Current nominal thickness range: 60 to 110 mm.
- Current standard widths: 430, 580 and, for one R2.7 product, 600 mm.
- Current standard lengths: 1160 mm, plus one R2.7 product at 1200 mm.
- The TDS describes the batts as firm, low-itch, easy to cut and suitable for friction fitting between compatible studs or joists.
- The current product is coloured green for identification; the manufacturer states that the colour and packaging change did not change performance or product codes.
- The manufacturer states the product is made in Australia from up to 80% recycled content.
- The TDS identifies the product as FBS-1 Glasswool Bio-Soluble Insulation.
- The current TDS reports non-combustibility to AS 1530.1.
- The current product page identifies CodeMark Certificate of Conformity CM 30006.

## Acoustic performance and correct interpretation

The current TDS publishes sound absorption coefficients measured to AS ISO 354 and the following summary values:

| Material R-value | Thickness | NRC | αw |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| R2.0 | 70 mm | 1.05 | 1.00 |
| R2.5 | 90 mm | 1.10 | 1.00 |
| R2.7 | 90 mm | 1.10 | 1.00 |
| R3.1 | 110 mm | 1.15 | 1.00 |

These are laboratory sound-absorption results for tested batt specimens. Values above 1.00 can occur in reverberation-room testing because the measured equivalent absorption area includes specimen-edge effects. The bot should reproduce the published metric and context; it should not convert it into a percentage or cap/change the result.

The TDS does **not** publish a standalone Soundbreak product `Rw`. `Rw` describes airborne sound reduction through a tested building element or construction, whereas NRC and αw describe absorption behaviour. A cavity batt cannot be assigned the completed wall's Rw.

The following spreadsheet values are therefore quarantined unless a matching system test is supplied:

- `Rw 40`, `Rw 45` and `Rw 50` attached to older Soundbreak rows;
- generic `NRC 0.75`, `NRC 0.80` or range values that conflict with the current TDS;
- any claim that a higher R-value automatically produces a specific wall Rw;
- any numerical comparison between Soundbreak NRC and NuWave product Rw.

## Thermal performance and correct interpretation

`R1.7`, `R2.0`, `R2.5`, `R2.7` and `R3.1` are manufacturer-published **material R-values** for the current products. They are not automatically the Total R-value of a completed wall, ceiling or floor.

The spreadsheet currently contains derived or legacy “Spec Total R-value” figures such as R1.87, R2.17, R2.42, R2.87 and R3.27. These are not supported as universal Soundbreak product ratings by the current TDS and must not be used without a defined construction and calculation source.

The TDS requires the product to recover fully and be installed without compression to retain the claimed material R-value. Cavity depth and batt thickness must therefore be checked before selecting a variant.

## Physical, fire and moisture properties

The current TDS reports:

- maximum service temperature: 340°C when tested using ASTM C411/C447;
- thermal-conductivity compliance: AS/NZS 4859.1;
- moisture absorption: less than 0.2% by volume under ASTM C1104;
- fungi resistance: pass/no growth under ASTM C1338-14;
- non-combustible classification under AS 1530.1;
- early-fire-hazard indices under AS/NZS 1530.3: ignitability 0, spread of flame 0, heat evolved 0 and smoke developed 1.

These are component properties under the cited tests. They do not prove the fire resistance level, acoustic rating or moisture performance of a complete building system.

Soundbreak is not a water or vapour barrier. The manufacturer says it must remain dry and must not be exposed to weather before, during or after installation.

## Compliance and BAL interpretation

The 2024 TDS references NCC 2019 and NCC 2022 provisions, AS/NZS 4859.1, AS 1530.1, AS/NZS 1530.3 and AS 3959. It also states BAL Low–FZ for walls, floors and ceilings.

For the bot, this means **documented component evidence exists**. It does not mean every construction containing Soundbreak is automatically NCC-compliant or suitable for BAL-FZ.

Before a compliance-facing recommendation or quote, a human reviewer must confirm:

- the applicable NCC edition and state/territory variation;
- building class and application;
- current certificate and product-code coverage;
- exact wall, ceiling or floor system;
- whether the project relies on a Deemed-to-Satisfy or Performance Solution pathway;
- current AS 3959 edition, BAL level and construction detail;
- any fire-resistance-level, acoustic or consultant specification.

## Application boundaries

### Good family-level fit

- Airborne voices, television or general room-to-room sound through framed residential walls.
- External airborne noise where Soundbreak is specified inside an appropriate external-wall system.
- Theatre/media-room, bathroom, laundry and kitchen adjacency where cavity absorption is required.
- Ceilings or midfloors where the complete system and lining weight limits are checked.
- Projects needing both acoustic absorption and a documented material R-value within limited cavity depth.

### Not the primary product role

- Impact noise from footsteps through a finished floor: investigate a tested floor/underlay/resilient system.
- Pipe, waste or duct breakout noise: investigate purpose-made acoustic lagging or duct-liner systems.
- A thin flexible mass layer behind linings: investigate an MLV/barrier family such as Thermotec NuWave or Fletcher Quadzero where supported.
- Reverberation/echo treatment within a room: concealed cavity batts are not automatically exposed acoustic panels.
- Water barrier, vapour control or external weather exposure: select an appropriate membrane system.
- Fire stopping: use the nominated tested fire-stopping or party-wall system component.

## Soundbreak versus Thermotec NuWave Base

These families perform different roles and should not be ranked by comparing their headline numbers.

| Selection question | Soundbreak | Thermotec NuWave Base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product mechanism | Porous glasswool absorption within a cavity | Flexible mass barrier added to a construction |
| Primary useful metric | Material R-value plus NRC/αw absorption data | Product Rw for the barrier specimen, with system caveat |
| Thermal contribution | Yes, documented material R-value | Not its primary documented role |
| Typical position | Between compatible studs/joists | Continuous layer, normally concealed behind lining |
| Space constraint | Requires appropriate cavity depth | Adds a comparatively thin mass layer |
| Can both appear in one design? | Yes, if supported by a tested/assessed complete system | Yes, if supported by a tested/assessed complete system |

The bot should favour Soundbreak when the customer needs cavity insulation with acoustic absorption and thermal value. It should favour NuWave when the problem and construction call for an added flexible mass barrier. It may surface both as complementary candidates but must not invent the performance of a combined system.

## Installation context for enquiry handling

Capture the following before selection:

1. Wall, ceiling or midfloor application.
2. New build or retrofit and whether the cavity is open.
3. Timber or steel framing.
4. Clear stud/joist spacing and cavity depth.
5. Proposed linings and any resilient channels or clips.
6. Noise type: airborne, impact, services, structure-borne or reverberation.
7. Thermal material/Total R-value requirement.
8. Acoustic target and source of that target.
9. Fire, NCC, BAL or consultant requirements.
10. Wet-area, condensation, weather or moisture exposure.
11. Approximate area, obstructions and service penetrations.
12. State/territory and supply location for current SKU availability.

### Installation controls

- Keep the product dry and protected from weather.
- Allow compressed packs to recover fully before installation.
- Match batt width and thickness to the actual framing and cavity.
- Friction fit without gaps while avoiding compression.
- Cut accurately around services and penetrations rather than folding or bunching.
- Check ceiling-lining manufacturer weight limitations for ceiling applications.
- Follow the current Fletcher installation guide and relevant safety information.
- Use appropriate PPE and dust controls when cutting or handling, following the SUIS.

## Approved customer-facing language

### Family recommendation

> Based on the information provided, this demo recommends the Fletcher Soundbreak family because the problem is airborne sound transfer through an accessible framed wall cavity and thermal performance is also important. Soundbreak is a high-density glasswool batt designed for acoustic absorption inside suitable walls, ceilings and midfloors. A team member still needs to confirm the wall construction, cavity depth and exact batt before quoting.

### Soundbreak versus NuWave

> Soundbreak and NuWave perform different jobs. Soundbreak is an absorptive batt installed within a cavity and also provides thermal resistance. NuWave is a flexible mass barrier that adds surface mass to a construction. Depending on the tested wall design, either or both roles may be relevant; we need the complete wall build-up before selecting the system.

### Acoustic rating question

> Fletcher publishes absorption ratings for Soundbreak batts, but the Rw of your finished wall depends on the complete construction. We cannot use the batt's NRC or material R-value as the wall Rw. If you have a target, the team will match it to a tested or assessed wall system.

### BAL or compliance question

> The current Soundbreak data sheet includes component fire and BAL information. Project compliance still depends on the exact application, construction, current code edition and supporting system evidence, so this must be checked before confirmation.
